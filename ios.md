# DocScan for iOS — Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 11 September 2026_

DocScan is a document scanner that runs entirely on your device. This policy
covers the iPhone and iPad app. The Android version is covered by a
[separate policy]({{ site.baseurl }}/), because the two apps use different
camera and text-recognition components.

## What DocScan collects

**Nothing.** DocScan has no account, no analytics, no advertising, and no
crash-reporting service. It does not send your documents, the text recognised
from them, or anything about how you use the app to the developer or to anyone
else.

The app makes no network requests of any kind. There is no DocScan server for
it to talk to.

## Where your documents are stored

Your scans, the processed versions of them, and the text recognised from them
are stored inside DocScan's own app container on your device. Other apps cannot
read them.

Deleting DocScan removes everything in that container permanently, so unless
you have set up a backup folder (below) or restore from a device backup, there
is no other copy — export anything you want to keep before deleting the app.

You can delete any page or document from inside the app at any time.

## Device backups

DocScan's documents are stored in the app's Documents container, which means
they are **included in your iPhone or iPad backup** if you have iCloud Backup
or encrypted computer backups switched on. That is how your library survives
getting a new device.

Those backups are made and held by Apple under
[Apple's privacy policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/), not by DocScan.
The developer has no access to them. If you would rather DocScan's documents
were not backed up, you can turn iCloud Backup off for the whole device in
Settings.

## Backup folder, if you choose it

DocScan can mirror your documents to a folder **you** pick through the iOS
Files picker — iCloud Drive, Dropbox, a file provider, or a folder on the
device. This is off until you choose a destination.

When it is on, DocScan writes to that folder: a PDF of each document, the
original captured images, and an index file recording your folder names, page
order, chosen looks and the text recognised from your pages. That index is what
lets a new device rebuild your library.

The destination is your own storage. There is no DocScan account. Whatever
provider you picked handles that data under its own privacy policy, and you can
remove the destination at any time in Settings.

## Camera

Scanning uses Apple's built-in document camera (VisionKit), which runs on your
device as part of iOS. Captured images are handed straight to DocScan and saved
to your device. No third party is involved, and nothing about a scan is
reported anywhere.

DocScan asks for camera access the first time you scan. You can refuse, and use
"Import" instead.

## Text recognition

Text recognition uses Apple's on-device Vision framework. It runs on your
device, works with no network connection, and the recognised text is stored
only on your device, where it is used solely to let you search your own scans.

## Sharing

Documents leave your device only when you choose to share or export them — for
example exporting a PDF and sending it somewhere. At that point the app you
send it to handles it under its own privacy policy.

## Permissions

DocScan requests **camera access only**, and only when you first scan.

It does not request access to your photo library: importing uses the iOS
document picker, which gives the app only the specific files you pick. It does
not request location, contacts, microphone, or tracking permission, and it does
not use the Advertising Identifier.

## Children

DocScan is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children. It
collects no data from anyone, of any age.
